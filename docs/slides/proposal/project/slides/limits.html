Limitations of the implementation

Simulated traffic

Scripted users are more regular than real ones, so a real network would see more false alarms.

Simulated night-time

Off-hours activity is marked by a flag, not observed at night.

Small lab

One access switch and three users, all on one laptop with 16 GB of RAM.

Known attack types

Only five attacks were tested; others, such as lateral movement over SSH, were not.

Next: compare with autoencoders and One-Class SVM (Syzranova et al., 2026), and link alerts to a SOAR or ticketing platform (Kumar et al., 2025).

About 45 seconds. These are the limitations. All traffic is generated by scripts, which are more regular than real people, so the false-alarm rate on a real network would likely be higher. Night-time is simulated with a flag. The lab is small: one access switch, three users, and one laptop. And only the five attack types in scope were tested. To reduce the risk of a false alarm cutting someone off, unclear cases only raise an alert, and every action can be reversed. Future work is to compare Isolation Forest with other unsupervised models and to connect the response engine to a SOAR or ticketing platform.
