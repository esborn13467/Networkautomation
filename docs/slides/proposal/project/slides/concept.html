Introduction

Zero Trust that watches and acts

Most networks trust a device once it is inside. An attacker or a misused account can then move freely, and signature tools miss behaviour they have not seen before.

Connect

A user plugs in a PC. The port stays closed.

Verify

An 802.1X login is checked by RADIUS.

Segment

The role picks the VLAN; ACLs allow only what it needs.

Detect

Isolation Forest scores every PC every 10 s.

Respond

Block, quarantine, disable or alert. No person needed.

Never trust, always verify, then keep watching what each device does and act on it.

About 1 minute 10 seconds. The problem: most enterprise networks still trust a device once it is inside. If an attacker gets in, or a staff account is misused, little stops them moving between systems, and signature-based tools only catch attacks they already know. My concept combines Zero Trust with machine learning. Walk the picture left to right: a PC connects and the port stays closed. The user logs in with 802.1X and RADIUS checks the password. The user's role decides the VLAN, and ACLs allow only what that role needs. Once inside, the PC is still watched: every ten seconds its traffic is scored by an Isolation Forest. If it looks like an attack, the system responds by itself, using the same Zero Trust controls: block, quarantine, disable the account, or alert.
